## Timeline edits publish one revision snapshot

**confirmed** — `DomainCommandService.editContext` reuses the common revision path to mark the requested current revision `SUPERSEDED` and append a revision with the same logical ID and interval, incremented revision number, `TIMELINE_EDIT` kind, `USER_CONFIRMED` status, and no confirmation-answer fields. Its store-backed overload calls `ContextSnapshotStore.replaceAtomically(oldContexts, replacementContexts)` before returning the replacement state. `DomainCommandRegression.directTimelineEditAtomicity` verifies both the successful snapshot and a forced store exception that leaves the fake at its single, original current revision.

The no-store overload does not persist; it returns an immutable replacement `DomainState`, as documented. That preserves the pure calculation seam without providing an alternate storage side effect.

## Context confirmation and effective-decision priority remain intact

**confirmed** — `ContextRevision` still requires a confirmation answer for `CONFIRMATION` revisions, restricts alternate classifications to `OTHER`, and rejects confirmation provenance on a Timeline edit. `ContextEngine` considers only `CURRENT` plus `USER_CONFIRMED` revisions before automatic evidence, rejects multiple matches for a canonical segment, and lets the direct edit override the former automatic WASTE decision without including superseded WASTE in the resulting waste duration.

## Seven-day and Saved calculations retain fractional nanoseconds

**confirmed** — the baseline stores the seven-day total and represents its daily average as `Ratio(totalNanos, 7)`. `MetricsEngine` multiplies that rational by the supplied decimal day equivalent using the decimal's unscaled value and scale; subtraction and zero-clamping remain rational rather than truncating to a `Duration`. `AdditionalDomainRegression` demonstrates a 3,501 ns weekly baseline, an exact `3501/7` one-day expected value, an exact half-day value, the seven-day identity, and a negative fractional delta clamped to zero Saved time.

Recovery Rate converts `Duration` through seconds-and-nanoseconds into `BigInteger` components. Its sub-millisecond regression uses 250 ns recovered against 500 ns saved and observes the exact `250/500` ratio, while the status precedence still distinguishes unavailable rate reasons from the pending-overlap decision state.
